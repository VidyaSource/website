Legacy systems don't just slow down IT. They slow down every process built on top of them, from customer approvals to safety reviews, and Vidya has spent 6 years fixing that for federal systems that millions of people depend on. Organizations that hire Vidya for modernization get a system that keeps serving its current users while Vidya rebuilds the parts that are failing them, so customers see the old system, then gradually a faster one, with no gap in between.

The clearest proof point is [Online Passport Renewal](/blog/modernizing-online-passport-renewal-vidya-success-at-the-state-department) for the U.S. Department of State's Bureau of Consular Affairs, part of a modernization program Vidya has supported since 2019. That work cut passport renewal from months to days, and the most recent measurement puts customer satisfaction at 97% and the increase in public trust in the service at 80%, with more than 2 million renewals processed online. Vidya delivered a similar result at Recreation.gov, where a 6-month modernization tripled national park reservation volume, and at HealthCare.gov, where a technology overhaul improved performance by more than 30% while Vidya mentored more than 20 developers through the transition. On these programs, Vidya peels one function out of the legacy system at a time, runs the old and new versions side by side against the same live traffic, and only retires the old path once the new one has proven itself.

Vidya scopes a modernization engagement with a 4-week assessment that identifies your system's biggest risks and a sequenced plan for fixing them safely. You get a written roadmap and visible progress in production within the first quarter.

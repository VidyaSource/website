Vidya is an 8(a)-certified modernization firm that has spent three years helping business and government clients turn AI from a pilot project into a system their teams actually rely on. Clients who bring Vidya an AI project get something working inside their own operations within weeks, built around how the business already runs.

Most AI projects fail for reasons that have nothing to do with which model a company picks. MIT reported that 95% of enterprise AI deployments fail, largely from missing data strategy and unclear business goals. Vidya made the same case in public, arguing in [Python Is Not the Language of AI](/blog/python-is-not-language-of-ai) that succeeding with AI is a matter of giving a system the right business context, and Gartner's own research backs that up, finding that organizations who prioritize context can lift AI accuracy by up to 80% and cut costs by up to 60%. A simple test guides every AI recommendation Vidya makes: if the information an agent needs to do a task well can't realistically be made available to it, that task isn't ready for AI yet, and Vidya says so upfront. Where part of a job must produce the same correct answer every time, like a calculation or a compliance check, Vidya pairs the AI agent's language reasoning with a deterministic, rule-based step built for that part, so the pieces of the system that must be reliable stay reliable.

Vidya's founder was named to the inaugural cohort of 138 Ambassadors for the Agentic AI Foundation, the Linux Foundation-backed group that now governs the open standards behind today's leading AI tools, representing 41 countries. A stack built around one vendor's proprietary product fails the day that vendor deprecates it, ships a worse model, or loses an important certification. Vidya builds AI systems on open, vendor-neutral standards, so when a client needs a better tool later, swapping it in becomes a simple configuration change that protects the client's investment as the AI market keeps shifting.

Vidya scopes a first AI engagement as a two-week discovery sprint that identifies one high-value business process worth automating and maps the information it needs. At the end of those two weeks, you get a working system connected to your own data.
